floodfusion v1.2 · Arena

Every model, the same floods, and Copernicus's own maps as referee

Instead of asking is this pixel water?, v1.2 asks is this water unusual for this place and season? Here that idea meets its opponents on equal terms: a foundation model trained on the same labels, the operational Copernicus flood service, and a blind test on floods no model had seen, scored only after the models were frozen.

1 · Scoreboard

Copernicus EMS maps drawn on radar satellites, 2023–2026

Every model maps every flood zero-shot and is scored against the Copernicus EMS delineation of the same day. Five ways to score it: at the model's own threshold (τ*), the same on the binned scores, after a per-scene recalibration of the flood prior, at each flood's best threshold chosen in hindsight, and threshold-free (AP). A win that flips between them is not a win.

3 · Research landscape (October 2026)

Why a bigger network will not win, and what might

Numbers in this section are reported by others, on their own benchmarks; they are set apart from this project's results and cited.

The encoder is not the bottleneck

On GEOID-Flood's 219 Copernicus floods, every foundation model lands at 0.45–0.48 flood IoU, and a 32M-parameter Swin-T matches them. The authors conclude that training design matters more than the encoder; models trained on Kuro Siwo transfer almost as well as on their own set (0.54–0.57 against 0.59–0.60).

Chiriaco et al., GEOID-Flood, ECCV-W 2026. See also Tulbure et al., arXiv 2512.02055.

The real incumbent is operational

Copernicus GFM maps every Sentinel-1 scene with three algorithms and a majority vote, within hours, and its archive is open. Its next version (2027) adds a deep-learning member and hydraulic flood expansion. Beating a foundation model on a benchmark matters less than beating this.

CEMS, Global Flood Monitoring; Krullikowski et al., arXiv 2304.12488.

Retrospective benchmarks are running out

New training sets now cover the same Copernicus activations as this project's test set (GEOID-Flood reaches EMSR871; IBM and ESA's ImpactMesh covers hundreds of events). A model trained on them has seen the test. Only floods no model was trained or tuned on, scored after it is frozen, stay clean: hence the blind test below.

ImpactMesh (IBM, ESA Φ-lab).

Context helps more than capacity

What separates a flood from a dark field is what the place normally looks like: its radar a year earlier, its land cover, where water can flow. Land-cover priors improved every backbone on held-out floods, TerraMind included.

Thasma et al., arXiv 2606.29134; Tellman, ICLR 2026.

5 · Blind test

Floods no model had seen

Every eligible Copernicus flood activation from EMSR899 on (events from late July 2026) is scored by the frozen models, under rules committed before any of them was looked at. None was seen during development; each is scored only after the freeze. Scores stay sealed until the stopping rule is met: at least 20 activations in 15 clusters, or 31 December 2027. That detects gaps of about 0.06 IoU per activation and 0.07 per cluster; 10 activations would detect only about 0.10.
