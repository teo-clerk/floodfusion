# Data card

Everything `floodfusion` reads is public and needs no account. This card lists each source,
what it is used for, how it is obtained, its terms, and what is known to be wrong with it.
Nothing below is redistributed in this repository: data, chips, runs and checkpoints are
fetched or regenerated by the `make` targets.

## How the real data is assembled

- **One analysis grid per scene**: 10 m UTM; every layer is resampled once, by a kernel chosen
  by the quantity's type (bilinear for linear-power SAR and reflectance, nearest for classes).
- **Same-orbit pairs only**: the pre-event Sentinel-1 reference shares the post-event relative
  orbit and pass; an event without one is dropped, never matched across orbits.
- **Sen1Floods11 is re-ingested, not reused**: its images (S1 GRD processed elsewhere, S2 L1C, no
  pre-event scene) are replaced by Planetary Computer RTC pairs, S2 L2A and GLO-30 on the dataset's
  stated relative orbit and dates; only the labels are kept.
- **Kuro Siwo (v1.1) is re-ingested the same way.**
  - **Pass:** on each event's labelled date, the Sentinel-1 pass covering the most of the AOI; the event layer is
    rasterised on our grid.
  - **Chips:** per event, up to 12 wet (flood ≥ 1 %) and 4 dry 5.12 km squares, drawn by a seeded generator.
  - **Labels:** a second set has identical chips with the water label (event ∪ hydro).
  - **Splits:** events whose AOIs lie within 100 km form one split group.
- **The CEMS benchmark (v1.1) pairs each delineation with Sentinel-1.**
  - **Delineations:** only those drawn entirely on non-Sentinel images, with a published package.
  - **Pass:** a Sentinel-1 pass within ±24 h covering ≥ 50 % of the extent, plus a same-orbit pre-event pass.
  - **Tiles:** up to 16 per AOI and 4 AOIs per activation, drawn blind to the flood.
  - **Scored pixels:** the product's observed region, minus "not analysed" and permanent water.
- **Seasonal references (v1.1).** For every stack: the median of same-orbit passes within ±30 days of the same date a
  year earlier, with at least 3 passes. Otherwise it is recorded as insufficient, and the baseline keeps the recent
  reference (counted in the reports).

- **GEOID-Flood (v1.2) goes through Kuro Siwo's re-ingest.**
  - **Events:** activations before EMSR656 only, so neither the CEMS 2023–26 benchmark nor the blind test (≥ EMSR899)
    is seen in training. Codes Kuro Siwo already has are dropped.
  - **Pass:** GEOID-Flood's own post-event Sentinel-1 time (from its shard index); Planetary Computer's acquisition at
    that time covering the most of the AOI.
  - **Labels:** class 2 is flood; class 1 and JRC occurrence ≥ 80 % are permanent water; unmapped stays unlabelled.
  - **Chips:** Kuro Siwo's seeded rule, capped at 4 wet and 1 dry 5.12 km squares per event; a square needs ≥ 50 %
    of its pixels mapped.
- **The v1.2 training pool** (`data/pool.py`) lists the chips of both sets in one manifest, without copying a raster.
  Split groups are recomputed across both: events within 100 km of each other share a group, whichever set they come
  from.
- **Sidecars beside each stack (v1.2).**
  - `gfm.tif`: GFM's layers on the stack grid (nearest), with the item ids, asset hashes and `processing:version`.
  - `dem.tif`: GLO-30 elevation in metres, for TerraMind's DEM input.
  - `seasonal.tif`: the seasonal reference, for the context model.
  - `aef.tif`: AlphaEarth, 8 PCA components (deferred).

## Measured corrections and known problems

- **GLO-30 declares no nodata (v1.1 erratum 2).** Before v1.2, terrain past a 1° DEM tile edge was read as 0 m. Every
  terrain layer derived from it (slope, incidence, shadow, HAND) was wrong there.
  - **Affected:** 14 % of the CEMS benchmark's area; 6–8 % of the training stacks of Sen1Floods11 and Kuro Siwo.
  - **Fix:** `read_dem` now reads the gap as missing.
  - **v1.2 data:** Kuro Siwo and the CEMS benchmark are rebuilt (`data/kurosiwo_v12`, `data/cems_benchmark_v12`), and
    GEOID-Flood was ingested after the fix.
  - **v1.1 data:** kept unchanged, so v1.1's numbers can be regenerated (`reports/v1_1_erratum.md`).
- **GFM coverage of the CEMS benchmark.**
  - **Gaps:** 557 of 605 stacks have a GFM product. GFM has no product for Sentinel-1C frames before October 2025, so
    8 pairs have none.
  - **Versions:** 328 stacks are v3.1.0, 24 are v4.0.0 and 205 are v4.1.0.
  - **Coverage rule:** a unit counts for GFM only where GFM covers ≥ 95 % of its scored pixels.
  - **Where GFM maps nothing:** its exclusion mask (radar shadow, layover, dense vegetation, built-up areas) can cover
    most of a tile. A sensitivity analysis scores the non-excluded area only.

- **Sentinel-2 vs Sentinel-1 geometry, and label registration.** The 2016-2019 Sentinel-2 L2A
  passes sit 1-3 px (10-30 m) from the RTC geometry, mostly east-west, and the Sen1Floods11 labels
  follow the optical image. Uncorrected, a label drawn on the optical image handicaps every SAR-only
  model and flatters fusion. Three offsets are measured per event, pooled over the windows of every
  chip, each with an approximate 95 % interval from resampling chips (1000 resamples):
  - **optical**: SWIR (B11) structure against SAR structure, on clear windows only (label-free);
  - **label**: the label's water edges against the SAR's;
  - **label vs optical**: the label against the SWIR — whether the label was drawn on this image.

  An offset counts as a measurement with at least 12 chips and an interval within ±1 px: the
  decision is a whole-pixel shift, and an offset pinned to within a pixel picks one that beats no
  shift in expectation, while a few-chip estimate (±4 px) is refused. The bound was first ±0.5 px; on
  Ghana it refused two independent estimates agreeing on ~(1, 1) px (label (1.0, 0.95) ±0.88, SWIR
  (0.75, 1.05) ±1.03), and it was widened before the remaining events were measured and before any
  model ran. Labels drawn on the optical
  image move with it by one shift: the label offset if it is a measurement, else the optical one, else
  none. Labels measured to sit elsewhere (label vs optical >= 1 px) get their own shift and the optical
  keeps its own. On cloudy monsoon events the SWIR offset has too few clear windows and under-reads
  (India, Mekong, Bolivia and Sri Lanka kept labels 1-3 px off the SAR with it alone).

  **What using the labels can bias.** The labels of the held-out event (leave-one-event-out) and of
  the test chips (published split) help set the geometry of their own evaluation target. The shift
  aligns labels with SAR water edges, so SAR-based models gain from it; optical-only models do not,
  because labels and optical move together. The effect is bounded: two integers per event, applied
  only when the offset is a measurement and at least 1 px; no model, threshold, calibration or
  checkpoint choice sees it. Left uncorrected: the whole-pixel rounding (up to 0.5 px per axis) and
  measured offsets below 1 px. The label offset cannot separate geolocation error from genuine
  label-vs-SAR edge differences (flooded vegetation, the S1-S2 gap of up to two days: Somalia and Sri Lanka).

  **Independent checks** ([`reports/sen1floods11_registration.md`](reports/sen1floods11_registration.md)),
  none of which measures the label against the SAR after the shift (that is ~0 by construction):
  SWIR vs SAR after the shift (label-free); label vs SWIR (do the labels follow the optical image);
  split-half (the shift from half of the chips, scored on the other half); the shift from
  published-split training chips only; and recovery of a known synthetic shift. Results:
  - **Registered:** Ghana, India, Nigeria, Spain, Sri Lanka and USA. After the shift, the label-free check
    puts the optical within 0.5 px of the SAR (intervals at most ±0.85 px).
  - **Not registered:** Mekong (optical ~(0.8, 1.8) px off, ±2.3) and Somalia (~(1.3, 1.1) px, ±1.1);
    no estimate passed the gate.
  - **Uncertain:** Bolivia (±2.2 px), and Pakistan (~1 px in x after its SWIR-based shift; its labels
    give no usable estimate).
  - **Stability:** within an event, the label offset varies by up to ~1 px between chip subsets; the
    published-train-only shift differs by 1 px in x on India, Spain and Sri Lanka. The estimator
    recovers a known (2, -3) px displacement to within ~0.5 px, under-reading it slightly.
  - **Where estimates disagree:** where both exist, the label's water-edge offset reads larger in x than
    the SWIR one (India 2.45 vs 1.2 px), although the labels sit on the optical image. The two
    estimators see different edges, and which is closer to the true geolocation is not settled.

  Scores on Mekong, Somalia, Bolivia and Pakistan carry a residual misregistration that penalises
  SAR-based models.
- **Mixed Sentinel-1A/1B pairs** show a ~0.6 px (~6 m) azimuth offset; same-satellite pairs
  co-register to 0.0 px. Below one pixel, it is recorded and left.
- **Archive gaps.** Paraguay (orbit 68, 2018-10-31) is not in the RTC collection and is dropped.
  Pakistan's GRD annotations are missing; its geometry comes from the orbit model
  (`data/orbit_model.py`, validated to 0.74° worst-case incidence error) and is flagged in its metadata.
- **Label semantics.** Sen1Floods11 labels *water*, drawn mostly on Sentinel-2: water under canopy
  is under-represented, and cloud-covered pixels are unlabelled. Flood = labelled water minus JRC
  permanent water. CEMS delineations are generalised maps from very-high-resolution imagery at a
  stated time and with their own minimum mapping unit: agreement with them measures two products
  at two instants, not ground truth.
- **Grids without a usable window** (the label or the swath covering too little of a chip) are
  dropped and counted in the ingest report.
- **Kuro Siwo (v1.1).**
  - **Yield:** 36 of 43 events produced chips (3,971 windows, 29 split groups).
  - **Excluded events and why:**
    - Cameroon, Djibouti and Nicaragua: no date found;
    - USA (EMSR1111013) and Myanmar (EMSR130): no same-orbit pre-event pass within 60 days;
    - Ireland and the Netherlands: no pass on the labelled date covers ≥ 10 % of an AOI.
  - **Optical:** 209 of 452 stacks have a Sentinel-2 image within ±2 days.
  - **Registration:** the rule is Sen1Floods11's. The label offset was a measurement on 15 of 60 AOIs, with shifts
    of up to 3 px; the optical offset on 4. The other AOIs are not shifted.
  - **Label sets:** the water label raises the mean flood share from 0.095 to 0.114 on identical windows.
- **CEMS benchmark (v1.1).**
  - **Census:** 233 of the 243 activation codes are public; 10 return HTTP 403 and cannot be assessed.
  - **What qualified:** 44 activations and 117 AOIs. The caps leave 90 pairs.
  - **What the tests use:** the 38 activations drawn on radar, excluding EMSR664.
  - **What the products are:** generalised maps from 1–3 m imagery, up to 24 h from the Sentinel-1 pass. Low IoU
    against them partly measures the difference between two products.
  - **Overlap with training data:** five pairs overlap Kuro Siwo training AOIs by place. A sensitivity analysis
    drops them.
- **Seasonal references.** The archive a year before the 2016–2017 Sen1Floods11 events is sparse:
  - Pakistan and Sri Lanka have no seasonal reference.
  - India has one on 5 of 68 chips, Somalia on 20 of 26.

  Chips without one keep the recent reference.
- **Co-registration QA reports "not measurable", not "failed", where it cannot measure.** Each check
  (pre/post SAR, terrain simulation, SAR vs SWIR) counts only when its median shift has a standard
  error of at most 0.5 px (overlap-corrected). On the release data's null case — same-orbit pre/post
  pairs, which agree by construction — grids within that bound read >= 1 px in 2 % of cases, the
  others in 36 %. Flat terrain (the Po plain of EMSR664) gives the terrain simulation nothing to
  match, and is now reported as such.
